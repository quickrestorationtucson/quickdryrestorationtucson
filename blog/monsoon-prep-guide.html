Tucson Monsoon Home Prep Checklist 2026 | Quick Dry Restoration Tucson

Complete pre-monsoon checklist for Tucson homeowners. Roof, flashing, drainage, HVAC condensate, and insurance — everything to do before July.

Why Monsoon Prep Matters More in Tucson Than Anywhere Else

Tucson's monsoon season runs July through September — and it gives almost no warning. A storm can develop in hours and deliver 3 inches of rain in 30 minutes. Unlike most U.S. cities, Tucson's caliche soil (an impermeable hardpan layer just below the surface) cannot absorb that water. It runs off immediately, finding the path of least resistance — which is often straight into your home.

The homes that sustain the least damage every monsoon season are the ones where owners checked these things in May and June — before the first storm revealed the vulnerabilities.

1. Roof Inspection — Do This in May

Foam Roofs (Most Common in Catalina Foothills)

Walk the surface or hire a foam roofing contractor to inspect for cracks, voids, exposed seams, and deteriorated elastomeric coating. Foam roofs without adequate coating are vulnerable to even light rain penetration — and the Foothills gets significant hillside runoff concentration during a storm.

Red Clay Tile Roofs (Common in Oro Valley)

Look for cracked, chipped, or missing tiles. Check that the underlayment beneath the tiles is intact along edges and at transitions — the underlayment degrades over time even when tiles look perfect from the street.

Flat Membrane Roofs

Inspect for bubbling, separation at seams, and cracking — particularly around drains, HVAC units, and penetrations. Clear all roof drains and scuppers of debris before the season. A clogged drain on a flat roof can turn 2 inches of rain into a structural failure.

2. Window and Door Flashing

This is the #1 source of hidden stucco wall moisture damage in Tucson. Inspect all window and door perimeters for:

  • Gaps or cracks in caulk at window corners — especially on west and south-facing exposures
  • Separating flashing at the junction between window frames and stucco
  • Efflorescence (white mineral deposits) on stucco exterior surfaces — a sign water has been getting in

Recaulk any gaps with exterior caulk rated for Tucson's temperature range (40°F nights to 115°F days in summer).

3. Drainage and Grading

  • Ensure ground grade slopes away from your foundation on all sides — not toward it
  • Clear all downspout extensions and ensure they discharge water at least 4 feet from your foundation
  • Identify low spots in your yard where water pools and address with drainage before the season
  • Know where the arroyos and drainage channels are relative to your property

4. HVAC Condensate System

Tucson AC units run 14–18 hours per day from May through September — producing up to 30 gallons of condensate daily. When the condensate drain line clogs (which happens every few years as algae accumulates), that water overflows into your ceiling.

  • Schedule your annual AC tune-up in April or May
  • Ask your HVAC technician to flush the condensate drain line with bleach solution
  • Confirm your secondary condensate pan float switch is operational

5. Insurance Review

This is the one most homeowners skip — and the one that matters most when disaster hits.

  • Review your homeowners policy to understand exactly what monsoon damage is and isn't covered
  • If you're near an arroyo, wash, or low-lying area — consider purchasing NFIP flood insurance now. There is a 30-day waiting period, so do this in May or June before monsoon begins
  • Ask your agent about sewer/drain backup coverage if you don't already have it
  • Photograph your home's current condition for pre-loss documentation
